A one-shot blinking circuit, designed from schematic to fabrication-ready board in KiCad.

The brief was a timer circuit that fires once and blinks — simple enough to hold in your head, which is exactly what makes it a good first board. The interesting part was never the circuit. It was everything that surrounds a circuit once you decide it has to actually be manufacturable.

So the work went past "does it simulate" and into the things a fab house cares about. The board went through three revisions before it was signed off, and the artifacts from that process are the real record: separate front and back copper layers, solder mask, solder paste, silkscreen, and edge cuts, each exported as its own plot. A design rule check, run and archived as a report rather than glanced at and dismissed. A bill of materials generated from the schematic instead of retyped. A 3D render, because seeing the board with components standing on it catches things a flat layout never will.

That's the lesson worth keeping. **A schematic is a claim about what should happen; a fabrication package is a claim someone else can act on.** Learning the difference between the two — and learning that the second one is most of the work — was the point of the exercise.

It's also the most completely documented thing in this archive. Every gerber, every plot, the DRC report, the BOM and the renders are all in the repo. [Take a look at the board package](https://github.com/dmorales024/bme290) if you want to see what a finished one looks like.
