Thor's Hammer PCB

complete

My first PCB, and the enclosure I built to give it somewhere to live: Mjolnir.

This was BME290, an introduction to medical devices, and the half of it I remember is the hands-on half — CAD and ECAD, ending in a board of your own design and a box to put it in.

The circuit is a timer. Press the button and the lights blink out of phase with each other for somewhere between 500 and 1000 milliseconds, depending on where the potentiometer is set. Simple enough to hold in your head, which is what makes it a reasonable first board.

We designed the circuit and then built the entire board file in KiCad. I went one-sided, which makes routing harder and the result cheaper, and the layout turned out to be the part I enjoyed most. I like Tetris and I like Flow, and a single-layer board is the same game: everything has to fit, nothing may cross, and there is a solution in there somewhere if you are willing to move the pieces around enough times.

The enclosure brief was open — build whatever you like, as long as it holds the battery pack. I built Thor's hammer, partly because it would use every skill the class had covered and partly because the side panels gave me a reason to learn freeform sketching for the engraved artwork.

The board goes inside the head. The handle wraps in alternating bands, and the faces of the hammer carry the sketched artwork — the part of the project with no engineering justification whatsoever, which is why it is the part I would defend hardest.

I would not claim a grand lesson from this. What actually happened is that I stopped being nervous about KiCad, and these days I review other people's boards at the makerspace before they send them out for fabrication. The CAD is on Onshape if you want to pull the assembly apart yourself, and the gerbers, BOM and DRC report are all in the repo.
