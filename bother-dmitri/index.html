botherDmitri

complete

Press a button by my door and my phone makes a noise. That is the entire product.

The situation it solves is that I wear headphones and cannot hear anyone knocking. So the button goes where the person knocking is, and the sound comes out where I am. The alert is a metal-pipe-falling sound effect, which should tell you how seriously the project takes itself.

It is two halves: firmware on an nRF52833 development kit, and a SwiftUI companion app on my iPhone that scans for a peripheral advertising as BotherDmitri, connects, subscribes and plays the sound.

The part worth pointing at is that the Bluetooth service is a round trip rather than a one-way ping. One characteristic notifies the phone that someone knocked. A second one lets the phone write back after playback, confirming a noise actually came out. A notification only tells the firmware that a packet left the radio — it says nothing about whether the phone was awake, still connected, or audible. So the device lights a green LED only once the acknowledgement lands, and flashes red if nothing arrives within five seconds. The button tells you whether the knock got through, which is the difference between a doorbell and a doorbell you can trust.

Around 900 lines of C on Zephyr, split into a state machine, the knock service, LED control and button handling. It runs on the dev kit's onboard hardware alone — LEDs, one button, the built-in debugger over USB, no external parts.

With no screen and no serial console attached, the LEDs are the interface, so every state got its own pattern rather than a generic blink: slow red pulse waiting for the phone, pulsing yellow while sending, fast yellow awaiting acknowledgement, solid green for three seconds on success, double red flash on failure, solid red for a fault. The button does double duty — short press knocks, long press clears the error state. Presses while disconnected are ignored rather than queued, because a knock that cannot be delivered now is not worth delivering later.

Rough edges, stated plainly: the service UUIDs are still the placeholder 12345678-… pattern, which is fine for one device on a desk and not fine for anything real. The planning doc still describes a different target board, because the project started as a course scaffold and became this instead. It is four commits and a weekend.

It is BotherDmitri all the way down, incidentally — the Bluetooth advertising name, the bundle identifier, the app target. Source is public.
