complete

Press a button on a development board. My phone plays a sound. That is the entire product.

It exists because headphones work too well. Someone knocks, and I don't hear it. So the button goes where the person knocking is, and the noise comes out where I am. The alert is a metal-pipe-falling sound effect, which should tell you how seriously the project takes itself.

The interesting decision is that it's a round trip rather than a one-way ping. There are two characteristics on the custom Bluetooth service: one the device uses to notify the phone that someone knocked, and one the phone writes back to say the sound actually played. That second one is the point. A notification only tells the firmware that a packet left. It doesn't tell you the phone was awake, that the app was still connected, or that any noise happened. So the phone acknowledges after playback, the device turns its indicator green only when that acknowledgement lands, and if nothing comes back within five seconds it flashes red instead. The button tells you whether the knock got through — which is the difference between a doorbell and a doorbell you can trust.

The firmware is Zephyr on an nRF52833, running a state machine through boot, disconnected, and a connected cycle of idle, knock pending, then acknowledged or failed. There's no screen and usually no serial console attached, so the board's LEDs are the entire interface: a slow red pulse while it waits for the phone, yellow while sending, fast yellow awaiting the acknowledgement, green on success, a double red flash on failure. Every state got its own pattern rather than a generic blink, because a status light that means one thing is not a status light.

The button does double duty — a short press knocks, a long press resets out of the error state. Presses while disconnected are ignored rather than queued, on the grounds that a knock which can't be delivered now isn't worth delivering later.

The other half is an iOS app in SwiftUI and CoreBluetooth: scan for the device by name, connect, subscribe, play the sound, write the acknowledgement back.

It's a weekend build and the seams show — the service identifiers are still placeholders, which is fine for one device on a desk and fine for nothing else. The firmware and the app are both here.
